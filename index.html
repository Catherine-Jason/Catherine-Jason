About Me

I'm Catherine Jason, an IT and cybersecurity professional who works in security minded environments and enjoys solving problems that help keep systems stable and protected. I support technology operations for a 42-school district, where my day to day includes handling identity changes, reviewing endpoint activity, and helping maintain secure setups across thousands of devices, work that keeps me constantly exposed to live alerts, everyday user behavior, and the patterns that actually matter in security.

That exposure is backed by a technical foundation I've built through hands-on networking, Windows and Linux administration, and the security work I do in my own home lab. Outside of my full time job, I put in extra hours on cyber and SOC projects simply because this field excites me, not because anyone requires it of me. One of those projects is Honey-AI, a research honeypot I built to study how attackers try to manipulate AI chatbots, and much of my downtime goes toward analyzing logs, testing network behavior, and practicing forensic techniques so I can understand how issues start and how they move through a system. I like breaking things down, checking what's normal, and spotting the small details that don't fit, and that curiosity is what keeps me building even after a long day.

Right now that drive shows up in everything I'm juggling at once. I'm holding down a full time job while also running my own independent IT and cybersecurity consulting business on the side, helping small business and individual clients troubleshoot and secure their systems. Alongside that, I'm working through a competitive internship with the Cyber Ready Clinic and finishing the five remaining classes I need for my AAS in Cybersecurity, and I still find time to build my own projects, because this is something I genuinely want to get better at, not just something on a checklist. I hold Security+ and SOCOA I, and I'm currently working toward CompTIA Security AI+ to strengthen my understanding of AI-driven threats and defensive automation. Through the Cyber Ready Clinic internship, I learned the Discover5 and Ready5 assessment methods, took part in live assessments with nonprofit clients, and built my capstone, Click by Click Offboarding. Every class, every certification, every client I help through my own business, and every hour I put into the internship and my own projects adds another piece to how I think about security, and I look forward to all of it.

My goal is to move deeper into security operations and incident response. I've put in the work for it, the late nights after a full shift, the time spent helping clients through my own business, the hours on internship responsibilities, the evenings in my home lab, and the projects I built just because I wanted to understand something better, and I have no plans to slow down. I'm excited to bring that same drive to a team that values strong analytical thinking, steady growth, and someone who shows up ready to keep learning.

Click by Click Offboarding (Capstone)

My capstone for the Cyber Ready Clinic internship. I built a free web app that helps small nonprofits shut off access when staff or volunteers leave.

You answer a few questions about who is leaving and what they had access to, and it builds a checklist with only the steps that apply. If the exit is unplanned, the urgent steps like removing admin rights and blocking email sign in move to the top.

When you're done, it gives you a dated record for audits and cyber insurance. Everything runs in the browser and nothing is saved to a server.
